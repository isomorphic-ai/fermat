## 1. ELI5 — what this campaign actually is

**A proof does not destroy the unknown. It processes it.**

Most people picture a mathematical proof as a demolition: a big unknown stands there, the proof knocks it down, and at the end there is nothing left. This campaign is built on the opposite picture — the picture of a **bank ledger**. At every moment there is a fixed total of "open question." Each step of the proof takes some of that total and moves it from the *still-open* column to the *certified* column, and — this is the discipline — **the remainder is never deleted, rounded away, or forgotten**. It is retained, named, and carried forward. Every step must balance:

    total  =  processed (certified, with a receipt)  +  still-open (conserved, with a name)

The Lean structure at the bottom of the whole repo says exactly this (`Fermat/Conservation/Ledger.lean`):

    structure Ledger where
      stock credit converted total : α
      conservation : stock + credit + converted = total

A proof step is a `Transfer`: an accounted transaction with a `spent` field, an `available_eq` law, and a `total_preserved` law — you may move value between columns, you may never lose it. Later the campaign discovered that this ledger is literally the dimension formula of cohomology (**dim B + dim H + dim B_out = dim C** — "credit *is* cohomology"), and finally that it is the exact-sequence law of *observation itself*: for any readout `f`, retain `0 → ker f → V → im f → 0`. The campaign sentence: **conserve the fiber; process the quotient.** What your instrument reads is processed; what your instrument cannot see (`ker f`, the fiber) survives, on the books, unerased. The proof ends only when every entry of the input inventory has been processed — and an executable audit (`Verification.lean`, thousands of kernel-checked jobs, `#print axioms` frozen to the standard trio, `#guard_depends_on` on every public theorem) re-checks the balance at every station.

**Two more house metaphors you will meet below.** A **meter** is a covector — a thing that *reads* values (a voltmeter). A **probe** is a class — an actual element you can *insert* into a pairing (a test charge). They have opposite variance, and the single most important STOP in the campaign (Ulam W0) fired because the architecture had produced a beautiful meter where the task needed a probe; relabeling one as the other is the named failure mode, and the campaign refused. And a **receipt** is mechanical evidence — a named theorem, a green job count. In the audit table below, the *Processed* column contains only things with receipts.

**Why 59? The gauge idea.** Fermat's Last Theorem for exponent 59 is *already proven in this same repository* by the classical Vandiver–Takagi–Furtwängler route (`Fermat.holdsAt_fiftyNine` — kernel-checked, done). The conservation campaign locks that entire proof behind forbidden-import guards and asks a different question: *can the same territory be re-crossed so that every step is a conserving transaction?* So 59 is not the prize. **59 is the measuring instrument** — the first irregular prime that is small enough to compute with and irregular enough to be honest (a regular prime's credit ledger is the trivial vacuum; 59 actually has to borrow). The house rule makes this literal: every law is proved **generically over p** in the route-neutral core (`Fermat/Conservation/`), and 59 and its attestation prime 827 appear only in the instance layer (`Fermat/FiftyNine/Conservation/`). The deliverables are the invariants themselves — Ledger, Transfer, AreaTransfer, the cohomology ledger, the readout ledger — calibrated against 59 the way a new thermometer is calibrated against a known boiling point. The one theorem still open on this route, Vandiver's Lemma I ("relation 7a"), is the exact place where the instrument currently reads "not yet" — and the campaign's honesty rule is that it must *say* "not yet," in types, rather than fake a reading.

## 3. Walkthrough — one plain paragraph per row

**1. The charter.** Before anything, the campaign locked the vault: the finished classical proof of FLT(59) lives in this same repository, and an executable guard proves its every theorem name is *unknown* inside the new workspace. Then it minted the bank: a ledger with three columns (stock, credit, converted) and one law — the columns always sum to the total. Nothing may ever be proved by peeking at the old proof; everything must be re-earned as balanced transactions.

**2. The training wheels.** The proofs for exponents 1 through 7 were re-plumbed so that each one literally *runs through* the ledger — not "is consistent with" but "consumes in its proof value," checked by a dependency guard. This established the audit discipline on territory where the answers were already known: seven receipts, each on its own route, honestly recorded as *not yet* one common ledger.

**3. Opening the account.** An irregular prime is a prime that needs credit: its arithmetic "wants to borrow" against a class group. Station 3 measured the credit line. Using a bigger helper prime (827, which is *derived*, not chosen — it is the minimal certified step above 59 in the generation chain) as a lamp, a 28×28 table of readings was computed and kernel-checked to be invertible. Meaning: the account is finite, and its size shares no factor with 59. Bonus theorem: 59 does not divide the "plus" class number.

**4. Repayments clear.** Whenever the arithmetic takes a *deep* draw on the account (a unit congruent to a 59th power to very high depth), that draw is genuinely repaid — it *is* a 59th power. Crucially: the repayment happens in the *real-unit* currency. The ledger notes, honestly, that this currency is not the class-group currency where the big debt lives.
